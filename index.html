AI Tools for Biomedical Researchers: A Hands-On Workshop

Three weekly sessions that make graduate students and other learners, including postdocs and clinical residents and fellows on research rotations, comfortable using AI in their own research: how it works, how it fails, and how to put it to work on real data.

3 weekly sessions × 3.5 hours Dates: to be announced (same weekday, three consecutive weeks) Instructor: Marcus R. Breese, PhD

About the workshop

This workshop is designed for wet-lab researchers and is also useful for early-stage dry-lab students. You need very little Python and no prior AI or machine-learning experience. About two-thirds of every session is hands-on: you get comfortable by doing.

By the end, you can explain how AI fails, verify its output, connect your own data to an LLM, direct a coding agent through a full analysis, and apply rules for appropriate AI use in research.

One biological thread, three days

TP53 · MDM2 · nutlin

Day 1 predicts TP53 status from gene expression. Day 2 serves cell-line and literature data about p53 to an LLM. Day 3 analyzes the transcriptional response to nutlin, an MDM2 inhibitor, in TP53 wild-type vs. knockout cells.

The three days

Learning outcomes

By the end of the workshop, you can:

  1. Explain how classifiers and LLMs learn from examples, and predict where each one fails.
  2. Recognize and verify common LLM failures: fabricated citations, confident errors, and tokenization blind spots.
  3. Make your own data available to an LLM by building a retrieval index and an MCP server, and choose between prompt stuffing, retrieval, and tools.
  4. Direct a coding agent through a complete RNA-seq analysis from a written plan with pre-registered checks, and catch the errors the agent misses.
  5. Apply a framework for appropriate AI use in research, and write an AI-use disclosure.

How the sessions run

Do first, explain second.Each day opens with a hands-on task; the lecture explains what you just saw.
Use the tools, build the data side.You use provided AI interfaces and build only the pieces that connect your data: a classifier, a retrieval index, an MCP server.
Bring your own.Each day ends with you applying that day's tools to your own research.
Public data only.All workshop data is public; sensitive data stays in IU-approved tools.

Before you come

  • Complete a short survey: Python comfort, and whether you work with bulk or single-cell RNA-seq.

  • Appropriate AI use. The framework taught on Day 3 applies to all workshop work.

The rule behind the framework: AI can do the work, but you stay accountable for every result, citation, and word, and you disclose how you used it.

UseStatusCondition
CodingOKYou review it, test it, and can explain it
Orchestrating analysisOKYou own the plan and the checks; keep agent logs as provenance; no silent exclusions
